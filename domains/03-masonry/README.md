# Masonry — 10 items

Main source: *Modern Masonry* (8th or 9th edition, **study only**). In-room backup: IBC Ch. 21 (points to TMS 402/602), IRC R606–R608 and R703.8 (veneer), OSHA 1926.706.

## Key concepts

**Mortar types (ASTM C270).** Memory trick: **M a S o N w O r K**. Strongest to weakest:
| Type | Approx. min. compressive strength | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| M | 2,500 psi | Below grade, foundations, heavy loads, retaining walls |
| S | 1,800 psi | Structural, below grade, high lateral loads (wind, seismic) |
| N | 750 psi | General-purpose exterior above grade, veneer, parapets |
| O | 350 psi | Interior non-load-bearing |
| K | 75 psi | Historic restoration |

**Mortar handling.** Retemper (add water) only to replace evaporation. Discard mortar not used within **2½ hours** of mixing.

**Grout (ASTM C476).** Fine grout or coarse grout, placed at a slump of 8–11 in. Grout pours over about 5 ft high need cleanouts at the bottom of the cells (high-lift grouting).

**Units**
| Unit | Nominal | Actual | Per sq ft of wall |
|---|---|---|---|
| CMU | 8 × 8 × 16 in | 7⅝ × 7⅝ × 15⅝ in | 1.125 (112.5 per 100 sq ft) |
| Modular brick | 4 × 2⅔ × 8 in | 3⅝ × 2¼ × 7⅝ in | about 6.75 (675 per 100 sq ft) |

Standard mortar joint: ⅜ in. Nominal size = actual size + joint.

**Brick positions**
| Position | How it's laid |
|---|---|
| Stretcher | Horizontal, long face exposed (the most common) |
| Header | Horizontal, end exposed (ties wythes together) |
| Rowlock | On edge, end exposed (sills, caps) |
| Soldier | Standing on end, narrow face exposed |
| Sailor | Standing on end, wide (bed) face exposed |
| Shiner | On edge, wide (bed) face exposed horizontally |

**Bonds**
- Running bond: half-lap.
- Common (American) bond: a header course every 5th, 6th or 7th course.
- Flemish bond: alternating headers and stretchers in each course.
- English bond: alternating courses of headers and stretchers.
- Stack bond: no overlap, so it needs horizontal joint reinforcement.

**Joints.** Concave and V-tooled joints are the most weather-resistant because tooling compacts the mortar. Raked and struck joints hold water; avoid them on exterior walls exposed to weather.

**Movement**
- Clay brick expands over time (moisture), so it gets **expansion joints**.
- Concrete block shrinks, so it gets **control joints**.

**Problems**
- Efflorescence: white deposit of soluble salts carried to the surface by water. Fix the water source; dry-brush, then wash.
- Freeze-thaw spalling.

**Weather (TMS 602)**
- Cold-weather procedures apply below 40°F: heat materials and protect the work.
- Hot-weather procedures apply above 100°F, or above 90°F with wind over 8 mph: wet the units and limit how far mortar is spread ahead.

**Veneer (IRC R703.8)**
- 1 in minimum air space.
- Ties: each supports max 2.67 sq ft, spaced max 32 in horizontally and 24 in vertically.
- Weep holes: max 33 in o.c., at least 3/16 in diameter, above the flashing.

**Testing.** f'm is the specified compressive strength of masonry, verified by prism tests or the unit-strength method.

## Practice questions

### Y1
Which mortar type has the highest compressive strength?
- A) Type M
- B) Type S
- C) Type N
- D) Type O

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A** — Type M (about 2,500 psi). *Ref:* ASTM C270 / Modern Masonry

</details>

### Y2
The mortar type most commonly recommended for general exterior, above-grade, non-structural walls and brick veneer is:
- A) Type M
- B) Type S
- C) Type N
- D) Type K

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C** — Type N. *Ref:* ASTM C270 / Modern Masonry

</details>

### Y3
The actual dimensions of a nominal 8 × 8 × 16 CMU are:
- A) 8 × 8 × 16 in
- B) 7⅝ × 7⅝ × 15⅝ in
- C) 7½ × 7½ × 15½ in
- D) 7¾ × 7¾ × 15¾ in

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B** — Nominal size minus the ⅜ in mortar joint.

</details>

### Y4
About how many 8 × 16 in nominal CMUs are needed per 100 sq ft of wall?
- A) 90
- B) 100
- C) 112.5
- D) 125

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C** — 144 ÷ (8 × 16 = 128) = 1.125 per sq ft, so 112.5 per 100 sq ft.

</details>

### Y5
About how many modular brick are needed per square foot of single-wythe wall with ⅜ in joints?
- A) 4.5
- B) 5.5
- C) 6.75
- D) 7.5

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C** — About 6.75 (675 per 100 sq ft).

</details>

### Y6
A brick laid vertically on end with its narrow (stretcher) face exposed is called a:
- A) Rowlock
- B) Sailor
- C) Soldier
- D) Header

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C** — Soldier. *Ref:* Modern Masonry (brick positions)

</details>

### Y7
A brick laid on its edge with the end exposed, often used for sills and wall caps, is a:
- A) Rowlock
- B) Shiner
- C) Soldier
- D) Stretcher

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A** — Rowlock.

</details>

### Y8
Which mortar joint profiles give the best weather resistance?
- A) Raked and struck
- B) Concave and V
- C) Flush and weathered
- D) Extruded and beaded

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B** — Concave and V. Tooling compresses the mortar and sheds water. *Ref:* Modern Masonry

</details>

### Y9
Mortar should be used and placed in its final position within ___ of initial mixing.
- A) 1 hour
- B) 1½ hours
- C) 2½ hours
- D) 4 hours

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C** — 2½ hours. *Ref:* TMS 602 / Modern Masonry

</details>

### Y10
Efflorescence on a brick wall is caused by:
- A) Excess Portland cement in the mortar
- B) Water-soluble salts carried to the surface by moisture
- C) Freeze-thaw cycling
- D) Too many weep holes

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B**. *Ref:* Modern Masonry

</details>

### Y11
In anchored masonry veneer, weep holes must be spaced no more than:
- A) 24 in o.c.
- B) 32 in o.c.
- C) 33 in o.c.
- D) 48 in o.c.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C** — 33 in o.c., at least 3/16 in diameter, immediately above the flashing. *Ref:* IRC R703.8.6

</details>

### Y12
Each veneer tie may support no more than ___ of wall area.
- A) 2.0 sq ft
- B) 2.67 sq ft
- C) 3.5 sq ft
- D) 4.5 sq ft

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B** — 2.67 sq ft (max 32 in horizontal and 24 in vertical spacing). *Ref:* IRC R703.8.4.1

</details>

### Y13
Which statement about movement joints is correct?
- A) Brick shrinks, so it gets control joints
- B) CMU expands, so it gets expansion joints
- C) Brick expands, so it gets expansion joints; CMU shrinks, so it gets control joints
- D) Neither material needs movement joints if it is reinforced

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C**. *Ref:* Modern Masonry

</details>

### Y14
Masonry laid in stack bond requires:
- A) Type O mortar
- B) Header courses every sixth course
- C) Horizontal joint reinforcement
- D) Weep holes in every head joint

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C** — Stack bond has no overlap, so joint reinforcement is needed for continuity.

</details>

### Y15
Cold-weather masonry construction procedures are required when the ambient temperature falls below:
- A) 32°F
- B) 40°F
- C) 45°F
- D) 50°F

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B** — 40°F. *Ref:* TMS 602 / IBC 2104

</details>

### Y16
Grout for masonry is specified by:
- A) ASTM C270
- B) ASTM C476
- C) ASTM C90
- D) ASTM C150

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B** — ASTM C476. (C270 = mortar, C90 = load-bearing CMU, C150 = Portland cement.)

</details>
