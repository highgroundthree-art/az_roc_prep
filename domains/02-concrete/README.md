# Concrete — 14 items (largest domain)

Main source: *Design and Control of Concrete Mixtures* (PCA, 2011 or 2016 edition, **study only**). In-room backup: IBC Ch. 19 (points to ACI 318), IRC R402.2, R403, R506, and OSHA Subpart Q.

## Key concepts

**Portland cement types (ASTM C150)**
| Type | Use |
|---|---|
| I | Normal, general purpose |
| II | Moderate sulfate resistance, moderate heat |
| III | High early strength |
| IV | Low heat of hydration (mass concrete) |
| V | High sulfate resistance |

Arizona soils are often high in sulfates, so Type II/V cement is common here.

**Water-cement ratio (w/c).** This is the single biggest factor in strength and durability. Lower w/c means higher strength, lower permeability and better durability (Abrams' law). Adding water on site to raise slump lowers strength.

**Supplementary cementitious materials (SCMs):** fly ash (Class F, Class C), slag cement, silica fume. They improve long-term strength and sulfate resistance and lower heat.

**Admixtures**
| Admixture | Does |
|---|---|
| Air-entraining | Microscopic bubbles for freeze-thaw resistance (typically 4–8% air) |
| Water reducer / plasticizer | Same slump with less water |
| High-range water reducer (superplasticizer) | Flowing concrete, very low w/c |
| Retarder | Slows set (hot weather, long hauls) |
| Accelerator | Speeds set (cold weather). Calcium chloride is the classic one, but it promotes corrosion of reinforcing steel and is not used in prestressed concrete |

**Aggregates.** Maximum size is the smallest of: 1/5 of the narrowest form dimension, 1/3 of the slab depth, or 3/4 of the clear spacing between bars.

**Tests**
| Test | Standard | Key facts |
|---|---|---|
| Slump | ASTM C143 | 12 in cone; 3 layers, 25 rod strokes each; measures consistency/workability |
| Cylinders | ASTM C31 (make), C39 (break) | 6×12 in or 4×8 in; standard strength age is 28 days |
| Air content | ASTM C231 (pressure), C173 (volumetric) | Volumetric method for lightweight aggregate |
| Unit weight | ASTM C138 | Normal-weight concrete is about 145–150 lb/cu ft |
| Sampling fresh concrete | ASTM C172 | Take samples from the middle of the batch |

**Strength gain.** Roughly 65–70% of 28-day strength at 7 days, with proper curing.

**Curing.** Keep the concrete moist and warm. Methods: ponding, wet burlap, curing compound, plastic sheeting. Seven days is the common minimum for Type I.

**Hot weather (ACI 305).** Risk of rapid evaporation and plastic shrinkage cracks. Use cool water or ice, retarders, windbreaks, fog spray and evaporation control.

**Cold weather (ACI 306).** Applies when air temperature is or will fall below 40°F. Protect concrete from freezing until it reaches about 500 psi. Use heated materials, insulated blankets and accelerators.

**Placing and finishing.** Don't drop concrete from excessive height, which causes segregation. Consolidate with vibrators. Sequence: screed, then bull float, wait for bleed water to evaporate, then edge, joint, float and trowel. Never finish while bleed water is on the surface; it causes dusting and scaling.

**Joints**
- Control (contraction) joints: depth about 1/4 of slab thickness; spacing about 24–36 × slab thickness (for example, a 4 in slab gets joints every 8–12 ft).
- Isolation (expansion) joints: around columns and at walls.
- Construction joints: where a pour stops.

**Reinforcing**
- Bar number is the diameter in eighths of an inch (#4 = ½ in, #5 = ⅝ in).
- Grade 60 = 60,000 psi yield.
- Minimum cover (ACI 318): 3 in when cast against earth; 2 in for #6 and larger exposed to earth or weather (1½ in for #5 and smaller); ¾ in for slabs and walls not exposed.

**Volume math.** Cubic yards = (L × W × D in feet) ÷ 27.

## Practice questions

### C1
Which ASTM C150 cement type is specified for high early strength?
- A) Type I
- B) Type II
- C) Type III
- D) Type V

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C** — Type III. *Ref:* PCA Ch. 2 (Portland cements)

</details>

### C2
Concrete exposed to soils with high sulfate content should use:
- A) Type I cement
- B) Type III cement
- C) Type IV cement
- D) Type V cement

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D** — Type V (high sulfate resistance). *Ref:* PCA Ch. 2

</details>

### C3
Increasing the water-cement ratio of a mix will:
- A) Increase strength and reduce permeability
- B) Decrease strength and increase permeability
- C) Have no effect on strength
- D) Reduce shrinkage

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B** — More water means lower strength and higher permeability. *Ref:* PCA Ch. 9 (Designing and proportioning)

</details>

### C4
In the standard slump test, the cone is filled in ___ layers, each rodded ___ times.
- A) 2 layers, 20 times
- B) 3 layers, 25 times
- C) 4 layers, 25 times
- D) 3 layers, 35 times

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B** — 3 layers, 25 strokes each. *Ref:* ASTM C143 / PCA Ch. 16 (Control tests)

</details>

### C5
The standard age for testing concrete cylinders to verify specified compressive strength (f'c) is:
- A) 3 days
- B) 7 days
- C) 14 days
- D) 28 days

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D** — 28 days. *Ref:* ACI 318 / PCA Ch. 16

</details>

### C6
The primary purpose of air entrainment is to:
- A) Increase compressive strength
- B) Improve resistance to freeze-thaw damage
- C) Accelerate setting time
- D) Reduce the amount of cement

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B** — Freeze-thaw durability (it also improves workability). *Ref:* PCA Ch. 8 (Air-entrained concrete)

</details>

### C7
Calcium chloride is an admixture that:
- A) Retards set and is preferred for prestressed concrete
- B) Accelerates set but can promote corrosion of embedded steel
- C) Entrains air
- D) Reduces water demand by 12–30%

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B** — An accelerator. It increases the risk of corrosion of reinforcing steel and is not used in prestressed concrete. *Ref:* PCA Ch. 6 (Admixtures)

</details>

### C8
Under ACI 318, the minimum concrete cover for reinforcement in concrete cast against and permanently exposed to earth is:
- A) ¾ in

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D** — 3 in. *Ref:* ACI 318 cover table, referenced by IBC 1901.2

</details>

### C10
How many cubic yards of concrete are needed for a 30 ft × 40 ft slab 4 in thick (no waste)?
- A) 11.1 cu yd
- B) 14.8 cu yd
- C) 16.0 cu yd
- D) 44.4 cu yd

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B** — 30 × 40 × 0.333 ft = 400 cu ft ÷ 27 = 14.8 cu yd.

</details>

### C11
The nominal maximum size of coarse aggregate should not exceed:
- A) 1/3 the narrowest form dimension

- C) 1/2 the slab depth
- D) The clear cover to reinforcement

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B**. *Ref:* PCA Ch. 5 (Aggregates) / ACI 318

</details>

### C12
Control joints in a slab on grade should be cut to a depth of about:

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

</details>

### C13
Final finishing (floating and troweling) of a slab should begin:
- A) Immediately after screeding
- B) While bleed water is still on the surface
- C) After the bleed water has evaporated from the surface
- D) After 24 hours

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C** — Working bleed water into the surface causes dusting, crazing and scaling. *Ref:* PCA Ch. 13

</details>
